# ADR-002: Private Ownership with Anonymous Public Maps

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-07-31

## Context

Every field worker needs an isolated workspace, while selected maps should be useful to the public. The product needs an unambiguous relationship between ownership, visibility, discovery, and editing rights.

## Decision

Every map belongs to exactly one user and is private by default. Only its owner may create or edit it during normal use. The owner may publish or unpublish the map. Published maps appear in a searchable public gallery and have a shareable public route accessible without an account. Anonymous visitors may navigate the map and inspect element details and photos, but may not edit, export, or download its data. Unpublishing immediately removes public access.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Authenticated-only public access

- **Description**: A published map is visible only after login.
- **Pros**: Every viewer is identifiable.
- **Cons**: Adds friction and prevents genuinely public links.
- **Why rejected**: The requested behavior allows anyone to view without login.

### Alternative 2: Team sharing and collaborative editing

- **Description**: Owners invite other users to view or edit private maps.
- **Pros**: Supports team collaboration.
- **Cons**: Requires invitations, membership, and shared-edit conflict rules.
- **Why rejected**: The selected scope is owner-only editing plus public read-only access.

## Consequences

### Positive

- Private data is the safe default.
- Public maps are discoverable and easy to share.
- Editing authority remains simple and predictable.

### Negative

- Teams cannot privately collaborate on one map in this version.
- Public photos and element details become accessible to anyone while a map is published.

### Risks

- Owners may publish sensitive content accidentally; the publish action must clearly explain public exposure and require confirmation.
- Cached public pages may outlive unpublishing in clients; public routes must re-check current visibility.

## Implementation Notes

Authorization must be enforced for every map and element operation, not only hidden in the interface. Public map responses must expose only fields intended for anonymous viewers.

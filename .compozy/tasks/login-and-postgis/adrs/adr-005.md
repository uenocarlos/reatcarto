# ADR-005: Durable Geospatial Records and Complete Account Deletion

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-07-31

## Context

The current application stores map content locally. The requested product must persist user data and map elements centrally in PostgreSQL with PostGIS, including information collected in the field and attached photos.

## Decision

Use the server-side PostgreSQL database with PostGIS as the authoritative store for accounts, maps, and geospatial elements. Support point, line, and polygon elements with geometry, name, description, category, style, author, creation and update dates, and one or more photos. Keep offline copies as synchronized account-bound working data rather than an independent source of truth. Allow users to permanently delete their own account; confirmed deletion removes the account, maps, elements, photos, public access, active sessions, and pending account-bound server data.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Local storage as the primary store

- **Description**: Continue keeping maps only on the device.
- **Pros**: Works without a server.
- **Cons**: No cross-device access, durable ownership, public gallery, or central recovery.
- **Why rejected**: It does not meet the requested database-backed account model.

### Alternative 2: Deactivate accounts without deleting data

- **Description**: Preserve all user data indefinitely after deactivation.
- **Pros**: Easier recovery and historical support.
- **Cons**: Conflicts with the selected self-service permanent deletion behavior and increases privacy exposure.
- **Why rejected**: The user chose definitive deletion of all account data.

## Consequences

### Positive

- Users can recover their maps across devices and publish selected maps.
- Geospatial data remains queryable and consistently associated with its owner.
- Users control definitive removal of their account data.

### Negative

- Photos and geometries increase storage and synchronization demands.
- Permanent deletion cannot be undone after confirmation.

### Risks

- Cascading deletion may leave orphaned files or public references; deletion must be complete and observable.
- Large geometries or photos may degrade field synchronization; the product must communicate limits and progress.

## Implementation Notes

The TechSpec must define the schema, spatial reference handling, indexes, media storage, limits, backup/deletion semantics, and consistency between map ownership and element ownership.
